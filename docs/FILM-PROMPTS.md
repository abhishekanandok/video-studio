# Making a film with Drishyya: 8 ready topics (geography)

Open **Plan with Drishyya**: `http://localhost:3031/app/motion`

For each topic below, copy every block into its field, top to bottom, exactly as the page shows them:

| # | Field on the page | Where it is |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Topic** | Top box: "What is the video about?" |
| 2 | **Notes** | Big box: "Your notes (optional)…" |
| 3 | **Audience** | Small box, left: "Audience: e.g. class 10 students" |
| 4 | **Tone** | Small box: "Tone: e.g. curious, friendly" |
| 5 | **Language** | Drop-down: English / Hinglish / Hindi |
| 6 | **Length** | Drop-down: "Length: about 1 min" |
| 7 | **Format** | Button: "16:9 Video" (click once for "9:16 Short") |
| 8 | **Look** | **Auto** (the first card) lets Gemini pick the family from your topic. To choose yourself, filter with the chips (Print, Hand-drawn, Screen & tech, Photo-led, Poster & art) and click a card. Each topic below names the look that suits it best |

Then press **Plan it**. What to do after that is at the end of this file. When the storyboard is ready, the film page shows **This film's look**: press **Re-roll look** if you want different details (headlines, boxes, motion) in the same family.

The **FACTS** in each set of notes come from me. Check any number before you plan, because the film says it out loud.

## Topic 1: Why the world's best chips come from one island (Taiwan)

**1. Topic**
```
Why almost all of the world's most advanced chips are made on one island: Taiwan
```

**2. Notes**
```
FACTS (use only these):
- TSMC (Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company) was founded in 1987 by Morris Chang in Hsinchu, Taiwan.
- TSMC does not design chips. It makes chips that other companies design: Apple, NVIDIA and AMD are customers.
- NVIDIA's H100, the chip behind much of today's AI, is made by TSMC.
- Taiwan makes about 90% of the world's most advanced chips.
- Taiwan is about 36,000 square km, smaller than Kerala and Karnataka put together.
- The Taiwan Strait between Taiwan and mainland China is about 130 km wide at its narrowest.
- A single modern chip factory (a "fab") costs 20 billion dollars or more.
- TSMC is now building factories in Arizona (USA) and Kumamoto (Japan).

VISUALS:
- Open on a real map: zoom from the whole world down to Taiwan.
- A real photo of a TSMC building or a chip wafer, as a cut-out.
- Motion: chips designed in California travel to Taiwan to be made, then out to the world.
- A big number: about 90% of the most advanced chips.
- A real map of the Taiwan Strait with the 130 km measured across it.
- A big number: 20 billion dollars for one factory.
- A real map: new factories appear in Arizona and Japan.
- End with big words: ONE ISLAND, WHOLE WORLD.
```

**3. Audience**
```
curious people who hear about the chip race and Taiwan in the news but don't know why it matters
```

**4. Tone**
```
clear, a little dramatic
```

**5–9. Settings:** English · about 1 min · 16:9 Video · Look: **Atlas** (or Dark tech)

## Topic 2: The undersea cables that carry the internet

**1. Topic**
```
The internet runs under the sea: the cables that carry the world's data
```

**2. Notes**
```
FACTS (use only these):
- About 99% of the data that crosses oceans travels through cables on the sea floor, not satellites.
- There are more than 500 undersea internet cables in the world.
- In the deep sea, a cable is about as thick as a garden hose.
- Inside are glass fibres. Data travels through them as pulses of light.
- The first cable across the Atlantic was laid in 1858, for the telegraph.
- Cables are damaged about 150 to 200 times a year, mostly by ship anchors and fishing nets.
- Special ships repair them by pulling the cable up from the sea floor.
- Big tech companies such as Google, Meta, Microsoft and Amazon now own or share many of the new cables.

VISUALS:
- Open on a real world map: cable routes draw themselves across the oceans.
- A big number: 99% of data that crosses oceans.
- Motion: a cut-away of a cable, then light pulses racing through a glass fibre.
- A real photo of a cable-laying ship, as a cut-out.
- A big number: more than 500 cables.
- Motion: an anchor drags across the sea floor and snaps a cable.
- End with big words: THE INTERNET IS WET.
```

**3. Audience**
```
people who think the internet comes from satellites or "the cloud"
```

**4. Tone**
```
surprising, curious
```

**5–9. Settings:** English · about 1 min · 16:9 Video · Look: **Transit map** (or Atlas)

## Topic 3: Why data centers move to cold places

**1. Topic**
```
Why the world's data centers are moving to cold places
```

**2. Notes**
```
FACTS (use only these):
- Computers turn electricity into heat, and a data center full of AI chips gets very hot.
- In older data centers, cooling can use up to about 40% of all the energy the building uses.
- Data centers used about 1.5% of the world's electricity in 2024.
- Cold outside air and cold water can cool a data center for almost nothing.
- Meta runs a data center in Luleå, Sweden, near the Arctic Circle, cooled by outside air.
- Google's data center in Hamina, Finland, is cooled with seawater from the Gulf of Finland.
- Almost all of Iceland's electricity comes from renewable hydro and geothermal power.
- Microsoft tested a data center sealed in a tube on the sea floor off Orkney, Scotland, from 2018 to 2020.

VISUALS:
- Open with motion: a server rack glowing hot, heat waves rising.
- A big number: up to 40% of the energy goes to cooling.
- A real map: data centers appear in Sweden, Finland and Iceland.
- A real photo of the Luleå data center or Arctic Sweden, as a cut-out.
- Motion: cold seawater flowing through pipes past hot servers.
- A real photo of Iceland's geothermal power, as a cut-out.
- End with big words: COMPUTE GOES NORTH.
```

**3. Audience**
```
people curious about where AI actually runs
```

**4. Tone**
```
curious, clear
```

**5–9. Settings:** English · about 1 min · 16:9 Video · Look: **Field journal** (or Dark tech)

## Topic 4: The Strait of Malacca, Asia's narrow door

**1. Topic**
```
The Strait of Malacca: the narrow sea lane that much of the world's trade must squeeze through
```

**2. Notes**
```
FACTS (use only these):
- The Strait of Malacca runs between the Malay Peninsula and the Indonesian island of Sumatra.
- It is about 900 km long.
- Near Singapore, its shipping lane is less than 3 km wide.
- More than 80,000 ships pass through it every year.
- About a quarter of the world's traded goods pass through it.
- It is the shortest sea route between the Indian Ocean and the Pacific, linking India, the Middle East and Europe with China, Japan and Korea.
- Much of the oil that China, Japan and South Korea import comes through it.
- Chinese leaders have called their dependence on it the "Malacca dilemma".

VISUALS:
- Open on a real map: ships flowing from the Indian Ocean towards East Asia.
- Zoom in on the Strait of Malacca on a real map.
- A big number: less than 3 km wide near Singapore.
- A real photo of container ships near Singapore, as a cut-out.
- A big number: more than 80,000 ships a year.
- Motion: the lane narrows and the ships queue up.
- End with big words: ONE NARROW DOOR.
```

**3. Audience**
```
students and curious people who like maps
```

**4. Tone**
```
clear, a little dramatic
```

**5–9. Settings:** English · about 1 min · 16:9 Video · Look: **Atlas** (or Transit map)

## Topic 5: Why China controls the metals inside AI hardware

**1. Topic**
```
Why China controls the metals inside AI hardware
```

**2. Notes**
```
FACTS (use only these):
- Rare earths are a group of 17 metals. They are not actually rare, but they are hard to separate and refine.
- Neodymium, a rare earth, makes the strongest permanent magnets: they are in motors, fans and hard drives.
- China mines about 60% of the world's rare earths and refines about 90%.
- China produces about 98% of the world's raw gallium, a metal used in some chips and electronics.
- In 2023, China announced export controls on gallium and germanium.
- The Mountain Pass mine in California was once the world's biggest source of rare earths.
- Building a refinery outside China takes many years.

VISUALS:
- Open with a real photo of a rare earth mine, as a cut-out.
- A real map: China lit up as the main source.
- A pie chart: China refines about 90%, everyone else about 10%.
- Motion: ore goes from mine to refinery to magnet to a server fan.
- A big number: 98% of raw gallium.
- A real photo of the Mountain Pass mine in California, as a cut-out.
- End with big words: WHOEVER REFINES, RULES.
```

**3. Audience**
```
people following the AI and chip race in the news
```

**4. Tone**
```
serious, clear
```

**5–9. Settings:** English · about 1 min · 16:9 Video · Look: **Documentary** (or Constructivist)

## Topic 6: Why India gets the monsoon

**1. Topic**
```
Why India gets the monsoon
```

**2. Notes**
```
FACTS (use only these):
- In summer, land heats up faster than the sea.
- Hot air over the land rises, and moist air from the Indian Ocean rushes in to take its place.
- That moist wind is the southwest monsoon.
- The monsoon usually reaches Kerala around 1 June.
- It usually covers all of India by about 8 July.
- About three-quarters of India's yearly rain falls between June and September.
- The Western Ghats and the Himalayas force the moist air to rise, cool and fall as rain.
- Mawsynram in Meghalaya gets about 11,000 mm of rain a year, one of the wettest places on Earth.

VISUALS:
- Open on a real map of India and the Indian Ocean.
- Motion: the land glows hot, the sea stays cool, arrows of wind sweep in from the sea.
- A real map: the monsoon line moves up from Kerala (1 June) to all of India (8 July).
- A pie chart: about three-quarters of the year's rain falls in June to September.
- Motion: moist air climbs the Western Ghats and turns into rain clouds.
- A real photo of rain over the Western Ghats or Meghalaya, as a cut-out.
- A big number: 11,000 mm a year in Mawsynram.
- End with big words: THE SEA COMES INLAND.
```

**3. Audience**
```
class 8 to 10 students, and anyone who never understood the monsoon
```

**4. Tone**
```
curious, friendly
```

**5–9. Settings:** English · about 1 min · 16:9 Video · Look: **Field journal** (or Atlas)

## Topic 8: How the Himalayas are still rising

**1. Topic**
```
How the Himalayas are still rising
```

**2. Notes**
```
FACTS (use only these):
- About 50 million years ago, the Indian plate crashed into the Eurasian plate.
- The crash folded and pushed up the rock between them: that is the Himalayas.
- India is still moving north, about 4 to 5 cm a year.
- So the Himalayas are still rising, by a few millimetres a year.
- Mount Everest is officially 8,848.86 metres tall (measured in 2020).
- Fossils of sea creatures are found high in the Himalayas, because that rock was once the floor of an ocean.
- The same push causes earthquakes: the 2015 earthquake in Nepal was magnitude 7.8.

VISUALS:
- Open on a real map: India drifting north across the ocean towards Asia.
- Motion: two plates collide, the land between them crumples and rises.
- A big number: 4 to 5 cm a year, still moving.
- A real photo of Mount Everest, as a cut-out.
- A big number: 8,848.86 metres.
- A real photo of a fossil ammonite, as a cut-out.
- End with big words: THE MOUNTAINS ARE STILL GROWING.
```

**3. Audience**
```
students and anyone who has seen the Himalayas
```

**4. Tone**
```
curious, a little awed
```

## After you press "Plan it"

1. **Wait about 4 minutes.** You get one storyboard card per line of the script.
2. **Read every card.**
   - Wrong words: press **edit words** on that card. Only that line is re-voiced.
   - Wrong picture: press **request a change** and say what it should show. For example: "Show the cache as a shelf of K and V cards, not a database icon." Only that card is redrawn.
   - In the log, read every **"Check:"** line. These are the fact checker's warnings; fix any line it flags.
3. Press **Approve all**, then **Write**. The film arrives in about 5 minutes.
4. If the log says Gemini is busy or rate-limited, **don't press anything**. It waits and retries by itself.
5. If you see **"Something is still being worked on"**, a job is still running for this film. Wait for it to finish.

## What you can ask for in VISUALS

Each line of the script becomes one printed spread. Write VISUALS so each one points at a layout:

| You want | Write it like this | Layout it becomes |
|---|---|---|
| One big number | "A number card: ₹1,20,000 you put in" | **number**: printed huge, counting up |
| A number growing over time | "Glass bars: ₹41k at year 5 … ₹5 lakh at year 20" | **number** with 3D glass bars on a dark board |
| Something that repeats | "Every month stamps ₹500 on a calendar" | **stamps**: a calendar of stamps |
| An acronym | "SIP spelled out, first letters lit" | **stamps** title with lit initials |
| A process | "A flow chart: Salary → Fund → Spending (crossed out)" | **steps**: 01 02 03, a coin travels between them |
| A count adding up | "240 squares filling, one per month" | **grid**: squares fill while the total counts |
| A split | "A pie chart: your money 24% vs growth 76%" | **pie** + legend + a dark board with the key number |
| Growth over time | "Bars per year: put in vs growth, with the multiple" | **combo**: paired bars, a ratio line, a callout box |
| A comparison | "Bars: India 10 billion vs Brazil 4 billion" | **bars** |
| Risk, ups and downs | "A market line going up and down" | **walk** |
| A punchline or ending | "End with big words: TIME DOES THE WORK" | **headline** |
| A real place or thing | "A real photo of the Bombay Stock Exchange" | **photo**, printed as a duotone |
| A place on Earth | "A real map: zoom from the world to Taiwan" | **map**: a real map that pans, zooms and draws routes |
| How a machine works | "Motion: a phone scanning a QR code" | **motion**: drawn by Gemini in the film's inks (at most 2 per film) |

## Rules that make a film good

1. **Facts first, only facts you're sure of.** The script is built on them.
2. **One idea per visual.** "A flow chart of the two phases", not "explain inference".
3. **Real photos only of real things.** "A sabzi mandi" works; "inflation" doesn't.
4. **Numbers with units.** "0.5 MB per token", not "a lot of memory".
5. **End with 2–4 big words.**
6. **Start with 1 minute.**
