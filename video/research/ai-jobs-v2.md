# Research pack v2: "Will AI take your first job?" (a real documentary: world + India)

Checked 29 Sep 2026. Every line below names its source; primary sources (company filings and memos, central banks,
official reports, peer-reviewed papers) are marked [P]. Claims that could not be traced to a reliable source are listed
at the end and must not be used. Attribution matters: say WHO claims what (e.g. Amazon's memo does not blame AI; its CEO's
earlier memo does).

## 1. Cold open: 2025-2026, companies say the quiet part out loud

- Oracle's annual report to the SEC (10-K, fiscal year to 31 May 2026) [P]: "The adoption and deployment of AI technologies
  across our operations have resulted, and may continue to result, in reductions to our workforce." Headcount 162,000 -> 141,000
  (about 21,000 fewer). Restructuring cost $1.84B. Capex +162% to $55.7B; about $70B targeted for FY2027.
  (SEC filing orcl-20260531; CNBC 23 Jun 2026)
- Block, 26 Feb 2026: about 4,000 jobs, cutting the company from 10,000+ to under 6,000 (about 40%). Jack Dorsey:
  "Intelligence tools have changed what it means to build and run a company." "A significantly smaller team, using the
  tools we're building, can do more and do it better." "Within the next year, I believe the majority of companies will reach
  the same conclusion." He said it was not financial distress. (CNBC, CNN, TechCrunch 26 Feb 2026)
- Amazon, 28 Jan 2026 [P]: about 16,000 corporate roles (about 30,000 since October 2025, the largest cut in its history).
  Its memo gives the reason as "reducing layers, increasing ownership, and removing bureaucracy" and does NOT mention AI.
  The AI link comes from CEO Andy Jassy's memo of 17 Jun 2025 [P]: "We will need fewer people doing some of the jobs that are
  being done today, and more people doing other types of jobs" ... "in the next few years, we expect that this will reduce our
  total corporate workforce as we get efficiency gains from using AI extensively across the company."
  (aboutamazon.com; CNBC; GeekWire)
- Salesforce: CEO Marc Benioff (Logan Bartlett Show, early Sep 2025): support headcount "from 9,000 heads to about 5,000,
  because I need less heads." He called it a rebalance; many were moved to other roles. (CNBC, Fortune 2 Sep 2025)
- Also in 2026, with AI named (TechCrunch running list, 25 Jul 2026): Meta ~8,000 (10%), PayPal 4,500+ (20%), Intuit ~3,000
  (17%), Snap ~1,000 (16%: "Rapid advancements in artificial intelligence enable our teams to reduce repetitive work"),
  Microsoft ~4,800 (2.1%, which said the roles are "not being replaced by AI" but "AI is changing how work gets done").
  Monday.com (~600) said its cuts were NOT to replace people with AI. Use the exact wording per company.
- Scale: Financial Times analysis: US tech companies cut nearly 140,000 jobs in 2026 so far (reported via Yahoo). Treat as
  "according to the FT".

## 2. We've been here before

- Luddites, Nottinghamshire, 1811: letters from "General Ned Ludd"; over 200 stocking frames destroyed in three weeks. The
  Frame-Breaking Act 1812 made machine-breaking punishable by death. (UK National Archives, "The proclamation of Ned Ludd" [P])
- John Maynard Keynes, "Economic Possibilities for our Grandchildren", 1930 [P]: "We are being afflicted with a new disease of
  which some readers may not yet have heard the name, but of which they will hear a great deal in the years to come - namely,
  technological unemployment." ... "unemployment due to our discovery of means of economising the use of labour outrunning the
  pace at which we can find new uses for labour. But this is only a temporary phase of maladjustment."
- "The Triple Revolution", open memorandum to President Lyndon Johnson, 22 March 1964 [P]: warned that "cybernation"
  (automation) would break the link between work and income. Johnson then signed the bill creating the National Commission on
  Technology, Automation, and Economic Progress; its 1966 report [P] concluded the US was not on the "verge of a glut of
  productivity sufficient to make our economic institutions and the notion of gainful employment obsolete."
- Telephone operators: at the late-1940s peak AT&T employed more than 350,000 operators, 98% women (Richmond Fed; ETHW).
  Feigenbaum & Gross, Quarterly Journal of Economics, 2024 [P]: AT&T mechanised switching in over half the US network
  (1920-1940). The jobs vanished, but later cohorts of young women were not less employed: they moved into clerical and new
  service jobs. The operators who lost their jobs WERE hurt: a decade later more were in lower-paid work or not working.
  (The honest lesson: the economy adjusts; the people in the job at the time pay.)
- ATMs: James Bessen (IMF Finance & Development, 2015) [P]: about 400,000 ATMs installed in the US (1980s-2010) while bank
  tellers grew from about 500,000 to nearly 600,000; tellers per branch fell from about 21 to 13, so banks opened more branches.
  Later, mobile banking did shrink teller jobs.
- Radiology: Geoffrey Hinton, 2016: "People should stop training radiologists now." Radiology has since faced a shortage
  (reported by Fortune, May 2026; the Fortune page could not be read by the agent, so keep the claim general).

## 3. What's different this time

- Cost: a16z "LLMflation" (Nov 2024): for the same capability, AI inference got about 10x cheaper per year; GPT-3-level output
  $60 per million tokens (Nov 2021) -> $0.06 (late 2024). Epoch AI [P-data]: matching GPT-4 on PhD-level science questions got
  about 40x cheaper per year; declines range 9x-900x per year by task.
- Capability: OpenAI's GDPval (published 25 Sep 2025; paper arXiv 2510.04374 [P]): 44 occupations across the 9 sectors that
  contribute most to US GDP, tasks written by professionals with ~14 years' experience, graded blind by experts. Best model,
  Claude Opus 4.1: 47.6% of its work rated as good as or better than the expert's; GPT-5 (high): 38.8% (figures as reported by
  36kr/AINews from OpenAI's post; the paper says "approaching industry experts" and "improving roughly linearly").
- Warning from inside the industry: Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei to Axios (28 May 2025): AI could eliminate half of all
  entry-level white-collar jobs within five years and push US unemployment to 10-20%. Many economists expect less.
- Counterweight: MIT Project NANDA, "The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025" (Aug 2025): 95% of enterprise generative
  AI pilots show no measurable P&L impact; $30-40B of enterprise spending; based on 150 interviews, 350 employee surveys and
  300 public deployments.
- Usage: Anthropic Economic Index (Jan 2026) [P]: in Nov 2025, 52% of Claude.ai conversations augmented work and 45%
  automated it (Jan 2025: 56% vs 41%).

## 4. Follow the money

- February 2026 earnings guidance (CNBC 6 Feb 2026): Amazon about $200B of 2026 capex; Alphabet $175-185B; Meta $115-135B;
  Microsoft on pace for about $145B. Together nearly $700B, more than 60% above 2025. Amazon's free cash flow may turn about
  $17B negative (Morgan Stanley) or $28B (Bank of America).
- Oracle: capex $55.7B in FY2026 (+162%), about $70B targeted for FY2027 (10-K [P]).
- About 75% of hyperscaler capex goes to AI infrastructure (Futurum). Amazon, Alphabet and Microsoft are set to spend about
  102% of their cloud revenue on capex in 2026 (Yahoo Finance analysis).
- The same companies are cutting jobs while spending: the money moves from people to data centres (Oracle's 10-K: savings
  redirected to AI data centres).

## 5. Is it a bubble?

- Sam Altman, OpenAI CEO (dinner with reporters, Aug 2025, first reported by The Verge): "Are we in a phase where investors as
  a whole are overexcited about AI? My opinion is yes." "When bubbles happen, smart people get overexcited about a kernel of
  truth."
- July 2026: after Alphabet's report, investors questioned rising capex; Amazon, Meta and Microsoft faced "skeptical
  investors" (CNBC 28 Jul 2026; page blocked to the agent: keep to the headline claim).
- Dot-com: Nasdaq peaked 10 March 2000 at about 5,048 and fell 78% by October 2002 (bottom about 1,114-1,141 depending on
  measure) (Britannica; Goldman Sachs history). In the Bay Area four of five dot-coms closed in 2000-2001.
- Two scenarios to lay out honestly: if the spending pays off, the automation continues; if it does not, a crash cuts jobs
  across tech anyway (the 2001 lesson). Either way, the entry level is exposed.

## 6. The first rung

- Stanford Digital Economy Lab (Brynjolfsson, Chandar, Chen), revised 12 Aug 2026, ADP payroll data [P]: employment of
  22-25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed jobs is ~19% below trend vs less-exposed peers (15% in Jul 2025); Nov 2022-Jun 2026:
  -11% in the most exposed jobs, +10% in the least exposed; mainly fewer hires, not firings; no economy-wide displacement;
  descriptive, not causal.
- Federal Reserve Bank of New York [P], 2026 Q2: recent college graduates (22-27) unemployment about 5.6% (5.7% in June 2026),
  underemployment 42% (working in jobs that don't need a degree).
- Counter-evidence: the New York Fed's own analysis points to remote work, not AI, as a bigger driver of young graduates'
  unemployment (NPR 18 Aug 2026; CNBC 25 Sep 2026). Yale Budget Lab: no discernible AI disruption of the overall US
  occupational mix since ChatGPT (Jan 2026).

## 7. India

- ILO & Institute for Human Development, India Employment Report 2024 [P]: graduate unemployment 29.1% vs 3.4% for people who
  cannot read or write; youth are ~83% of the unemployed.
- NASSCOM Strategic Review 2026 [P]: tech sector revenue ~$315B in FY26 (+6.1%) but headcount only +2.3% (+135,000, to
  ~6 million): revenue grows faster than jobs.
- TCS: announced ~12,000 cuts (2% of workforce, mostly middle and senior) on 27 Jul 2025 (Reuters); headcount 584,519 on
  31 Mar 2026, down 23,460 in FY26 (Deccan Herald; Zee Business).
- The top end is holding: IIT Kanpur's 2025-26 Day 1: 672 offers, +16% (IIT Kanpur [P]); Careers360: offers up 10-20% across
  IITs with salaries flat; more students rejecting pre-placement offers. Naukri JobSpeak: fresher (0-3 years) hiring +16% YoY
  in March 2026.
- So the Indian story is a split: elite campuses and AI-skilled freshers are hired; the broad mass of graduates faces a hard
  first step, and IT services no longer hire at the scale they did.

## 8. What a graduate should actually do (evidence only)

- WEF Future of Jobs 2025 [P]: fastest-growing skills: AI and big data, then networks and cybersecurity, then technological
  literacy. Top core skills employers name: analytical thinking (69%), resilience/flexibility/agility (67%), leadership (61%),
  creativity (57%). 77% of employers plan to upskill staff; two thirds plan to hire for AI skills.
- Stanford: where AI complements the work, young employment is flat or rising; where it automates, it falls. So: choose work
  where you direct the AI, not work the AI does alone.
- IBM plans to triple entry-level hiring for AI roles even while cutting elsewhere (TechCrunch list).

## 9. Verdict material

- Keynes' "temporary phase of maladjustment" vs the operators who paid the price; the money flowing from payrolls to data
  centres; the first rung is where it shows first. Both the fear and the hope are real.
