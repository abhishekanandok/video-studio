## NOTES (paste into the notes box)

THE EVIDENCE THAT SOMETHING IS HAPPENING (to young workers, not to everyone)
- Stanford Digital Economy Lab, "Canaries in the Coal Mine? Six Facts About the Recent Employment Effects of Artificial
  Intelligence" (Erik Brynjolfsson, Bharat Chandar, Ruyu Chen), revised 12 August 2026, using ADP payroll data (US).
  - Workers aged 22-25 in the most AI-exposed occupations: employment is about 19% below where it would be had it kept pace
    with same-age workers in less-exposed jobs. That gap was 15% in July 2025 and 19% by June 2026, so it is widening.
  - Between November 2022 and June 2026, employment of 22-25 year-olds in the two most-exposed fifths of occupations fell
    about 11%, while in the less-exposed fifths it grew about 10%.
  - Examples of exposed occupations: early-career software developers and customer-service workers.
  - It happens mainly through FEWER YOUNG PEOPLE BEING HIRED, not through people being fired. The first rung disappears.
  - Declines are where AI automates tasks; where AI complements workers, employment is flat or rising.
  - So far it shows up in jobs, not in base pay.
  - The authors' own caveats: "we do not see widespread, economy-wide job displacement associated with AI"; these are
    descriptive patterns, not causal estimates; some trends predate generative AI; the gap shrinks when education is
    accounted for.

THE CAUSE UNDERNEATH: INTELLIGENCE IS GETTING CHEAP, FAST
- Andreessen Horowitz (a16z), "Welcome to LLMflation" (November 2024): for a model of the same capability, the price of
  LLM inference fell about 10x per year over three years. In November 2021 GPT-3 was the only model scoring 42 on the MMLU
  benchmark, at $60 per million tokens; by late 2024 the cheapest model reaching that score cost $0.06 per million tokens:
  1,000x cheaper in three years.
- Epoch AI (data insight, "LLM inference prices have fallen rapidly but unequally across tasks"): the price of reaching a
  fixed benchmark level fell between 9x and 900x per year depending on the task; to match GPT-4 on PhD-level science
  questions, it fell about 40x per year.
- Why this matters for first jobs: the tasks entry-level workers are hired for (drafting, summarising, first-pass code,
  answering routine customer questions, data entry) are exactly the tasks that got cheap first.

THE BIG PICTURE NUMBERS
- IMF, Kristalina Georgieva, 14 January 2024: almost 40% of global employment is exposed to AI; about 60% of jobs in
  advanced economies. Roughly half of exposed jobs may benefit (AI boosts productivity); for the other half AI may perform
  key tasks now done by humans. In most scenarios AI will likely worsen overall inequality.
- World Economic Forum, Future of Jobs Report 2025 (January 2025; 1,000+ employers, 14 million+ workers, 55 economies):
  by 2030, 170 million jobs created and 92 million displaced, a net gain of 78 million; disruption equal to 22% of jobs.
  40% of employers expect to reduce their workforce where AI can automate tasks; 77% plan to upskill workers; two thirds
  plan to hire people with AI skills. Nearly 40% of job skills are expected to change. Fast-declining: clerical and
  administrative roles; fast-growing: technology, data and AI roles, but also care, education, delivery and farm work.

INDIA
- ILO and Institute for Human Development, India Employment Report 2024 (March 2024): unemployment among graduates was
  29.1%, versus 3.4% among people who cannot read or write. Youth are almost 83% of India's unemployed, and the share of
  the unemployed with secondary or higher education rose from 35.2% in 2000 to 65.7% in 2022. (This is before generative
  AI: Indian graduates already faced a hard first step.)
- TCS announced on 27 July 2025 that it would cut about 2% of its global workforce, about 12,000 roles, mostly middle and
  senior management; the company said the cuts were about skills and redeployment, not AI productivity.
- TCS headcount at 31 March 2026 was 584,519, down 23,460 over the year (FY26).
- Indian IT is still hiring freshers, but increasingly filtering for AI, cloud and data skills.

THE HOPE (and the limits of the fear)
- Yale Budget Lab (with Brookings), tracking the US labour market since ChatGPT (November 2022): no "discernible
  disruption"; the change in the occupational mix is not faster than earlier technology waves and most shifts were already
  underway in 2021; measures of AI usage show no clear link to changes in employment or unemployment (update published
  January 2026).
- Anthropic Economic Index (January 2026 report): in November 2025, 52% of conversations on Claude.ai were "augmentation"
  (the person learns, iterates or gets feedback) and 45% were "automation"; in January 2025 it was 56% vs 41%, and in
  August 2025 automation was briefly ahead. People mostly use AI WITH their work, but automation is a real and growing share.
- History, ATMs: economist James Bessen (Boston University) documented that as about 400,000 ATMs spread across the US
  (1980s-2010), bank-teller jobs grew from roughly 500,000 to nearly 600,000: ATMs cut tellers per branch from about 21 to
  about 13, which made branches cheaper, so banks opened more of them, and the teller job shifted to relationship work.
  Later, mobile banking did shrink teller jobs. Automation changes jobs before it removes them, and not always in the way
  predicted.
- Radiology: in 2016 Geoffrey Hinton said "People should stop training radiologists now". A decade later radiology faces a
  shortage and rising demand (reported by Fortune, May 2026).
- What protects a first job (from the sources above): work where AI complements rather than automates; skills employers
  say they are hiring for (AI, data, cloud, plus judgement and people skills); being the person who directs the AI.

## FACTS (paste into "Verified facts the film may cite")

- Stanford Digital Economy Lab (Brynjolfsson, Chandar, Chen), Aug 2026, ADP data: employment of 22-25 year-olds in the most AI-exposed jobs is ~19% below trend vs less-exposed peers (15% in July 2025).
- Same study: Nov 2022 to Jun 2026, young-worker employment fell ~11% in the most-exposed jobs and grew ~10% in less-exposed ones.
- Same study: the drop comes mainly from fewer young hires, not firings; authors see no widespread economy-wide displacement.
- a16z "LLMflation" (Nov 2024): same-capability AI got ~10x cheaper per year; $60 per million tokens (GPT-3, Nov 2021) to $0.06 (late 2024).
- Epoch AI: matching GPT-4 on PhD-level science questions got ~40x cheaper per year.
- IMF (Georgieva, 14 Jan 2024): ~40% of global employment exposed to AI; ~60% in advanced economies; about half of exposed jobs may benefit.
- WEF Future of Jobs Report 2025: 170 million jobs created and 92 million displaced by 2030 (net +78 million); 40% of employers expect to cut staff where AI automates tasks.
- ILO India Employment Report 2024: graduate unemployment 29.1% vs 3.4% for illiterate people; youth ~83% of India's unemployed.
- TCS, 27 Jul 2025: cutting ~12,000 roles (~2% of workforce), mostly middle and senior management.
- TCS headcount 584,519 on 31 Mar 2026, down 23,460 in FY26.
- Yale Budget Lab: no discernible AI disruption of the US occupational mix since ChatGPT (Jan 2026 update).
- Anthropic Economic Index (Jan 2026): 52% of Claude.ai conversations augment work, 45% automate it (Nov 2025).
- James Bessen: US bank tellers grew from ~500,000 to ~600,000 while ~400,000 ATMs were installed (1980s-2010).
- Geoffrey Hinton, 2016: "People should stop training radiologists now." Radiology now faces a shortage (Fortune, May 2026).
